01 / Abstract

Learning where actions matter.

Action-conditioned video models can emphasize large static regions while giving too little learning weight to small interaction-critical changes. KineWorld uses commanded robot motion to guide both the video condition and the spatial allocation of future-RGB supervision.

Kinematic Transport Lifting (KTL) constructs camera-aligned transport fields from a robot rollout. Transport-Aware World Diffusion (TAWD) calibrates the resulting motion support on the video latent grid and produces normalized token weights. The weighting branch changes training-time supervision without adding inference parameters or sampling steps.

Method summary adapted from the KineWorld V4 manuscript. This page does not report numerical benchmark results.

02 / Method

From commanded motion
to targeted supervision.

Two connected components turn robot kinematics into a video condition and a learning signal.

A / KTL

Kinematic Transport Lifting

Roll out commanded robot actions, render robot-only frames and masks, then estimate image-plane displacement with RAFT. The visible-source mask restricts transport to robot pixels.

Action condition · training and inference
B / TAWD

Transport-Aware World Diffusion

Calibrate transport activity on the video latent grid, mix transport-focused and uniform distributions, and normalize weights to mean one for future-RGB flow matching.

Supervision allocation · training only
